# Needle and Metal Control

> Synthetic demonstration document — not an official factory procedure.

## Purpose and Scope

This procedure governs needle breakage handling and metal-detector scanning, the two controls most directly aimed at preventing metal contamination (DEF-MC) from reaching a customer.

## Needle Breakage Logging

Every needle breakage is logged with the machine, operation, and time, and all broken pieces are accounted for before the machine resumes production; a breakage with a missing piece holds the machine and the bundle currently on it until a search either recovers the piece or the bundle is scanned and cleared.

## Metal Detector Scanning

Every finished unit passes through a metal detector before packing; a unit that triggers the detector is treated as a critical defect (DEF-MC) finding and follows the Critical Defect Response procedure rather than being re-scanned repeatedly until it passes.

## Detector Calibration

Metal detectors are calibrated against a certified test piece at the start of every shift and after any maintenance; a detector that fails calibration is taken out of service immediately, and no unit may pass to packing without passing a calibrated detector.

## Preventive Practice

Needles are changed on a fixed schedule rather than only after a breakage, following the Machine Preventive Maintenance procedure, since a needle nearing the end of its service life is more likely to break and shed a fragment during normal operation.

## Related Procedures and Review

This procedure is the primary preventive control behind Critical Defect Response and works alongside Machine Preventive Maintenance, which schedules the needle changes that reduce breakage frequency in the first place. Detector calibration records and needle breakage logs are reviewed together monthly by the quality manager and the maintenance team to confirm the two controls are working as a system rather than independently. A rising trend in either measure at a specific line is investigated before it produces a confirmed critical defect, since both controls are meant to catch a developing problem ahead of a customer-facing failure. Both reviews are documented and kept alongside the critical defect investigation records they support, so an auditor can trace prevention and response together for any given line.
