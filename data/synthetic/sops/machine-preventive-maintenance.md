# Machine Preventive Maintenance

> Synthetic demonstration document — not an official factory procedure.

## Purpose and Scope

This procedure defines scheduled preventive maintenance for sewing and pressing machines used across every operation in the operation catalogue, at both KTN and BYG.

## Maintenance Schedule

Each machine type carries a fixed preventive maintenance interval covering lubrication, needle and tension checks, and attachment wear inspection; the schedule is set from the manufacturer's recommendation and shortened for machines running a consistently high-volume operation such as overlock (OL) or side seam.

## Unplanned Downtime

A machine that stops unexpectedly is logged with the fault symptom, the operation it was running, and the downtime duration; downtime is treated as unavailable operator time for that shift slot rather than folded into an operation's cycle-time observations, so it does not distort a time study.

## Return to Service

A machine returning from maintenance is checked against a short first-off sample before it resumes full production, similar in spirit to the changeover first-off check, since a maintenance action can itself introduce a new tension or alignment issue.

## Records

Maintenance records are kept per machine and reviewed by the IE engineer whenever a line's bottleneck escalation repeatedly points to an operation running on that machine, since a machine overdue for maintenance can present as a bottleneck rather than as a maintenance problem.

## Related Procedures and Review

Scheduled needle changes under this procedure are the main preventive input to Needle and Metal Control, and unplanned downtime recorded here is reviewed by IE whenever Bottleneck Escalation repeatedly points to an operation running on an overdue machine. Maintenance intervals are reviewed annually against manufacturer guidance and against actual breakdown history at both the Katunayake and Biyagama plants, and are shortened for any machine type showing a rising breakdown rate. Maintenance technicians record parts replaced at each service so a recurring part failure across multiple machines of the same type can be identified as a specification or supplier issue rather than treated as unrelated one-off events. This history is available to the IE engineer during bottleneck investigations, since a machine's maintenance record is often the fastest way to rule mechanical wear in or out as a cause.
