# Defect Catalogue

> Synthetic demonstration document — not an official factory procedure.

## Purpose and Scope

This catalogue defines the standard defect codes, names, and severities used consistently across inline inspection, final inspection, and quality reporting at both KTN and BYG.

## Major Sewing Defects

The catalogue's major sewing defects are: DEF-OS open seam, DEF-SS skipped stitch, DEF-BS broken stitch, DEF-SV shade variation, DEF-MO measurement out of tolerance, DEF-NH needle hole, and DEF-WL wrong label. Each is logged with a count against the inspection it was found in and, where identifiable, the operation responsible.

## Minor Defects

DEF-ST stain and DEF-PK puckering are classified as minor defects: they affect a unit's cosmetic quality but are counted separately from major defects when computing an inspection's disposition, since the approved policy's acceptance numbers are sensitive to defect severity.

## Critical Defect

DEF-MC metal contamination is the catalogue's only critical-severity defect. A single critical defect found during FINAL inspection fails the lot outright under the Final Inspection Demo Policy, regardless of how few other defects were found.

## Catalogue Maintenance

Adding, renaming, or reclassifying a defect code requires quality manager approval and is never done informally on an inspection form; using an undocumented defect code makes downstream defect-rate and DHU reporting inconsistent across the two plants.

## Related Procedures and Review

Every inspection procedure in this corpus, inline and FINAL alike, references this catalogue's codes rather than defining its own; a defect observed under a code not listed here is not valid and must be raised with the quality manager as a possible gap in the catalogue rather than logged under an approximate existing code. The catalogue is reviewed at least annually and whenever a new customer's quality requirements introduce a defect type not previously tracked, ensuring reporting stays comparable across seasons and across both plants. Historical defect-rate and DHU reports are never recomputed under a revised catalogue; a catalogue change applies to inspections recorded from its effective date forward, so past reports remain comparable to the rules that produced them at the time. Proposed additions are piloted on inline inspection for a full season before being adopted for FINAL inspection, so a new code's definition is proven workable before it affects shipment decisions.
