# Inline Inspection Procedure

> Synthetic demonstration document — not an official factory procedure.

## Purpose and Scope

This procedure covers inline quality inspection performed while an order is still being sewn on the line, as distinct from the final inspection performed once an order is complete.

## Inspection Frequency

An inline inspector samples finished units from each active line at least once per hour, checking measurements, seam quality, and label accuracy against the style specification. A line running a newly loaded style is sampled more frequently during its first hour, consistent with the first-off check in the Style Changeover procedure.

## Defect Logging

Every defect found is logged against its defect code and severity — for example an open seam (DEF-OS, MAJOR) or a skipped stitch (DEF-SS, MAJOR) — and, where the operation is identifiable, against the specific operation that produced it, so recurring defects can be traced back to a specific step rather than only to the line as a whole.

## Response to Recurring Defects

If the same defect code recurs on three or more consecutive inline samples from the same operation, the inspector notifies the line supervisor immediately rather than waiting for the next scheduled report, since continuing to run the operation unchanged risks producing a full bundle of defective units.

## Relationship to Final Inspection

Inline inspection results inform the line supervisor and IE engineer but do not by themselves determine an order's shipment eligibility; that determination is made only by a FINAL inspection against the approved quality policy, per the Final Inspection Demo Policy.

## Related Procedures and Review

Inline findings inform Bottleneck Escalation when a recurring defect coincides with an operation already flagged as a bottleneck, and feed the same Defect Catalogue used by FINAL inspection so defect codes stay consistent across inspection stages. Inline inspectors are rotated across lines periodically so that inspection standards do not drift to match a single line's habits, and their sampling records are reviewed by the quality manager weekly for consistency. New inline inspectors are paired with a senior inspector for their first two weeks so that scoring and defect classification stay consistent before they inspect independently.
