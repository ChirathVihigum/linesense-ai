# Critical Defect Response

> Synthetic demonstration document — not an official factory procedure.

## Purpose and Scope

This procedure defines the immediate response required whenever a critical-severity defect — currently only DEF-MC metal contamination — is found at any inspection stage, inline or FINAL.

## Immediate Actions

The inspector who finds a critical defect stops sampling that unit's bundle immediately, isolates the unit, and notifies the quality manager before continuing the inspection. The bundle the unit came from is held pending investigation rather than returned to the line.

## Investigation

The quality manager investigates the likely source: a broken needle tip, a metal trim defect noted under Trims and Accessories Control, or a needle-detection scanner failure, per the Needle and Metal Control procedure. The investigation is recorded against the order and, where identifiable, the operation and machine involved.

## Quality Hold

Any order with a confirmed critical defect finding is placed on quality hold immediately, following the Quality Hold and Release procedure, and cannot be released until the source is corrected and a fresh sample confirms no further critical defects.

## Zero-Tolerance Policy

Under the Final Inspection Demo Policy, the maximum number of critical defects tolerated in a FINAL inspection sample is 0; a single confirmed critical defect fails the lot regardless of the count of any other defect type found.

## Related Procedures and Review

This procedure connects directly to Needle and Metal Control, which describes the preventive controls this response investigates first, and to Quality Hold and Release, which governs how the affected order is held until the source is corrected. Every critical defect investigation is reviewed by the quality manager and, where a machine or needle-detector fault is implicated, by the team responsible for Machine Preventive Maintenance, so a mechanical root cause is fixed rather than only the immediate unit addressed. Investigation findings are shared across both plants when the suspected cause is a supplier-side trim issue, since a defective batch of metal fasteners is unlikely to have been delivered to only one location. Every confirmed critical defect and its resolution is summarised for the next quality review meeting so the pattern of findings over time, not only the single incident, is visible to plant leadership.
