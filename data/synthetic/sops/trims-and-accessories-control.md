# Trims and Accessories Control

> Synthetic demonstration document — not an official factory procedure.

## Purpose and Scope

This procedure covers control of small trims and accessories — buttons (M10 Button 18L), zippers (M11 Metal Zipper 7in), labels (M12 Care Label, M13 Brand Label, M14 Hang Tag), packaging (M15 Poly Bag, M16 Carton Box), and fasteners (M17 Elastic Tape 25mm, M18 Twill Tape, M19 Snap Fastener, M20 Drawcord) — from receipt through issue to the line.

## Piece Count Control

Unlike fabric, trims are counted rather than measured, and bundle counts are verified at receiving, at supermarket replenishment, and again at issue to the line. A piece-count discrepancy of more than one percent of the requisitioned quantity is reported to the storekeeper before the trims are issued.

## Metal Trims and Needle-and-Metal Control

Metal zippers, snap fasteners, and buttons with metal shanks fall under the Needle and Metal Control procedure in addition to this one; any damaged or loose metal component found during trims inspection is set aside and logged, not simply discarded, since it may indicate a supplier packaging issue affecting the rest of the carton.

## Label Accuracy

Care labels, brand labels, and hang tags are matched to the specific style and customer before issue; issuing the wrong brand label against an order is a wrong label defect (DEF-WL) and, for certain customers, a contractual non-conformance more serious than most fabric defects, so label matching is double-checked against the cutting ticket before a carton of labels leaves the store.

## Packaging Readiness

Poly bags and carton boxes are staged ahead of an order's expected final inspection date so that packing can begin immediately once the order passes FINAL inspection, per the Packing and Shipment Readiness procedure; packaging is never opened or prepared for an order that has not yet passed FINAL inspection.

## Related Procedures and Review

This procedure is read together with Needle and Metal Control for metal trims and with Packing and Shipment Readiness for packaging materials, since both depend on the same piece-count discipline described here. A recurring piece-count discrepancy from the same supplier is escalated to the storekeeper's supervisor as a possible supplier packaging issue rather than logged repeatedly as an isolated event. Label accuracy checks are reviewed with the quality manager whenever a new customer's label specification is introduced, since a wrong label defect found late is far costlier to correct than one caught at trims issue.
