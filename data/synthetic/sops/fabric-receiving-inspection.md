# Fabric Receiving and Inspection

> Synthetic demonstration document — not an official factory procedure.

## Purpose and Scope

This procedure governs the receipt and physical inspection of piece-goods fabric (materials M01 Cotton Pique Fabric, M02 Cotton Jersey Fabric, M03 Polyester Mesh Fabric, M04 Denim Twill Fabric, M05 Rib Knit Collar Fabric, and M06 Fusible Interlining) at both the Katunayake (KTN) and Biyagama (BYG) plants. It applies to every fabric lot arriving under a purchase order reference, whether the lot is consumed against a factory order such as PO-KTN-0012 or a demo order such as PO-DEMO-001. It does not cover trims and accessories, which are governed by the Trims and Accessories Control procedure.

## Receiving Procedure

On arrival, the storekeeper records the delivery note, supplier lot code, and quantity in the intake register before the fabric is moved into the quarantine area. Every incoming lot is created with status QUARANTINE and may not be issued to any line, nor counted toward available stock, until it has passed inspection. Rolls are physically counted and weighed against the delivery note; any count discrepancy greater than one roll is flagged to the storekeeper's supervisor before inspection proceeds.

## Inspection Sampling

A four-point fabric inspection is performed on a sample of 10% of the rolls in each lot, rounded up to the next whole roll, selected at random across the pallet rather than only from the top layer. Each sampled roll is inspected for shade variation (defect code DEF-SV), stains (DEF-ST), and physical width/weight tolerance. Defects are scored per the standard four-point system and logged against the roll number, not merely the lot, so a single damaged roll does not condemn an otherwise acceptable lot.

## Acceptance Criteria

A lot passes inspection when its weighted four-point score across all sampled rolls is at or below the mill-agreed threshold and no sampled roll shows a critical safety defect such as metal contamination (DEF-MC). Lots that fail are marked REJECTED and returned to the supplier; lots that pass are marked ACCEPTED and only then contribute to on-hand accepted balance. Partial acceptance (accepting some rolls of a lot and rejecting others) requires the quality manager's written sign-off.

## Documentation and Escalation

The inspection result, sampled roll numbers, and four-point scores are attached to the lot record before its status changes out of QUARANTINE. Any inspector who identifies a pattern of repeated shade variation across three or more consecutive lots from the same supplier must notify the quality manager, since this may indicate a dyeing process issue upstream rather than an isolated lot defect.

## Related Procedures and Review

This procedure works together with Material Lot Acceptance, which governs the formal status transitions once inspection is complete, and with Material Reservation Policy, which explains why only ACCEPTED lots ever contribute to a material's available balance. The sampling rate and acceptance threshold in this document are reviewed annually by the quality manager together with the sourcing team, and any change is issued as a new document version rather than an informal memo, so that inspectors always work from a single current version and any order inspected under a prior version can still be traced back to the rule that applied at the time. Inspectors new to the fabric store are walked through this procedure and the four-point scoring method during induction, and are shadowed by a senior inspector for their first two weeks before inspecting a lot independently.
