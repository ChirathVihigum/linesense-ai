# Style Changeover Procedure (Katunayake)

> Synthetic demonstration document — not an official factory procedure.

## Purpose and Scope

This procedure covers changing a Katunayake line from one style to another between orders, including machine setup, operator reassignment, and the checks required before the new style's first units are sewn.

## Changeover Checklist

Before the previous style's last bundle leaves the line, the supervisor confirms machine attachments and guides required by the new style's operation sequence are available, thread colour matches the new style's shade card, and the operator roster's skills cover every operation the new style requires.

## Downtime Allowance

Changeover time is planned as unavailable operator time for that shift slot and is not counted against either the outgoing or incoming order's standard minutes; a changeover that runs longer than its planned allowance reduces the slot's remaining available minutes for the incoming order and must be reflected in the capacity plan.

## First-Off Inspection

The first units of the new style are inspected inline before the line is allowed to run at full speed, checking measurements and seam quality against the style's specification. A first-off failure holds the line at reduced speed until the root cause is corrected, rather than allowing a full bundle to be produced against a known defect.

## Handover Record

The supervisor records the changeover start and end time, the outgoing and incoming order references, and the first-off inspection result, so IE can measure changeover time separately from productive cycle time when reviewing the line's balance and throughput.

## Related Procedures and Review

Changeover records feed into Line Balancing Method and Bottleneck Escalation, since a line that appears to have a poor balance index immediately after a changeover may simply still be settling into the new style rather than genuinely unbalanced. The planned downtime allowance for each style pairing is reviewed by the IE engineer whenever actual changeover time consistently exceeds the plan by a wide margin, since an outdated allowance understates a line's true available capacity for the shift. A style pairing that repeatedly overruns its allowance for the same reason, such as a specific attachment that is slow to fit, is a candidate for a dedicated changeover kit kept ready at the line rather than fetched from tooling stores each time.
